§ 00 · A NOTE ON THIS DOCUMENT

This is a working manual. It holds the visual constraints — color, type, grid, mark — and the rules of restraint that keep them from drifting. Use it. Where you find it weak, edit it.

561 stands on its own. Don't pair it with EFA in lockups, mastheads, or chrome — the meaning gets thinner each time you spell it out. Once per document, in body copy, is plenty. The cipher is the joke; jokes don't need a footnote on every page.

§ 01.1
Set rules — stacked variant (default)
When required
Every page-level masthead. Every poster, banner, and pamphlet cover. The first folio of every printed long-form artifact (≥ 4 pages).
When permitted
Once per page-level artifact in the masthead position. A second instance of the slogan on the same artifact is a defect, with the single exception of the colophon citation, which uses the inline variant (§ 01.3).
Geometry

Size --ems-type-d1 (128 px screen / 96 pt print) at the artifact's primary scale; step down to --ems-type-d2 (76 px / 54 pt) only when artifact width < 480 px / 100 mm.

Leading --ems-lh-d1 (0.85). Tracking --ems-tr-d1 (−0.04 em). Word baseline interval = cap-height + (cap-height × 0.05). At 128 px, that is 96 px between baselines, ±1 px.

Optical alignment: left edge of every word flush to a single vertical line. The word "Water." typically requires +1 px optical pull-left to compensate for the round W counter. Verify on output.

Bracketing rules --ems-rule-bar (8 px / 2 mm) above the first word and below the last, full slogan width, color --ems-color-ink. Top rule offset --ems-u4 above the first word's cap-line. Bottom rule offset --ems-u4 below the last word's baseline.

Color

--ems-color-ink on --ems-color-paper, or --ems-color-paper on --ems-color-ink. No third ground.

One word may be set in --ems-color-alarm when that word is the artifact's specific subject — e.g., a poster about language sovereignty sets "Language." in alarm. The alarm word must change weight and color but not size; same --ems-type-d1, same --ems-tr-d1.

Never two alarm words in the same instance. Never alarm in the colophon variant.

Conflicts

If the slogan would render within --ems-u5 (48 px / 12 mm) of the mark (§ 02), the mark suppresses; only the slogan renders. The mark recurs in the colophon.

If a photograph or woodcut would overlap the slogan's bracketing rules, the imagery crops to clear them. Imagery never crosses an --ems-rule-bar.

Check
At --ems-type-d1 on a 30 cm reading distance, the alarm word must visibly differ from the ink words. If it does not, the alarm ink is under-saturated — reject the proof.
§ 01.2
Word order is load-bearing
Rule
The order is Land · Water · Language · Culture. It is not a list; it is a sequence of dependencies. Reordering changes the argument. Reordering is forbidden in any artifact bearing the 561 mark.
Translation
Translation is permitted into any language a 561 contributor reads or speaks. The translator's name and language pair must appear in the colophon (FIG 11.x). Translations preserve word order even when target-language syntax would prefer otherwise; if syntax cannot bear it, the translation is set as four separate sentences.
Abbreviation
No abbreviation, no acronym, no initialism. "L.W.L.C.", "LWLC", and "Land/Water/Lang/Cult" are all defects.
§ 01.3
Inline & contracted variants
Inline

Set as a single line: Land. Water. Language. Culture. Family Space Grotesk 700, size --ems-type-d3 (48 px / 36 pt) or smaller down to --ems-type-b1 (16 px / 10.5 pt). Tracking --ems-tr-d3.

Permitted in: colophons, sign-offs, footers, file metadata, body prose. Forbidden in mastheads.

Contracted

Permitted only at sticker/stamp scale (≤ 50 mm trim). Set as four periods on a baseline: . . . . One period per word, in original order. The mark (§ 02) must accompany; readers infer the slogan from the dot count.

Forbidden above sticker scale.

Forbidden variants
Centered set. Italicized set. Mixed-case set ("Land. Water. Language. Culture."). Any set without periods. Any set in a serif. Any set in alarm without a specific subject. Any rotation other than 0°.

§ 02.1
Primary mark — geometry & clearspace
When required
Every artifact 561 publishes. Letterhead, posters, pamphlets, the bible itself, the website, every public surface. Signage where 561 holds physical space.
When permitted

Once per page-level surface. Multiple instances on a single artifact are forbidden except in colophons (§ 11) where a small inline lockup may repeat.

The mark is not used to credit individuals. Individual contributors are credited by name in the colophon, not by the mark.

Geometry

Cap-height of the "5" defines unit H. All other measurements derive from H.

Numeral baseline-to-cap-line: H. Numeral-to-numeral interval: 0.06 H (negative tracking, optical not metric). Bracket rule offset: --ems-u2 (8 px / 2 mm) above the cap-line. Bracket rule weight: --ems-rule-bar (8 px / 2 mm). Bracket rule width: equals numeral block width, ±1 px.

Optical adjustments: the "1" sits 0.04 H right of metric center to balance the 6's overhang. Verify on every output medium; do not bake into the SVG source.

Clearspace

Minimum clearspace on all four sides: H (one cap-height of the numeral).

No type, no rule, no figure, no other mark may enter the clearspace box. The bracket rule above the numeral counts as part of the mark, not a violation of clearspace.

Trim or fold lines may pass through clearspace; nothing inked.

Color

Three permitted color states:

--ems-color-ink on --ems-color-paper (default, 95 % of usage).
--ems-color-paper on --ems-color-ink (dark surfaces, signage, second-color print runs).
--ems-color-alarm on --ems-color-paper (stamp variant only — § 02.3).

Forbidden: alarm on ink, witness on anything, the mark on a witness ground, the mark in any tint or screen of ink (no 50 % gray "subtle" mark).

Minimum size

Screen: H ≥ 32 px (numeral cap-height). Below 32 px the bracket rule disappears below sub-pixel rendering — use the contracted variant (§ 02.4).

Print: H ≥ 8 mm. Below 8 mm at 100 % scale, switch to the contracted variant or scale up the artifact.

Embroidery, debossing, fabrication: H ≥ 24 mm; bracket rule weight floors at 1.5 mm regardless of --ems-rule-bar spec.

Conflicts

The mark and the slogan (§ 01) never share a horizontal axis at masthead scale. If they would, the mark drops to the artifact's foot. The slogan owns the masthead.

If a photographic figure must occupy the same column as the mark, the mark moves to the opposite column or the foot. The mark never sits over a photo.

If the artifact carries a co-organizing partner's logo (e.g., a coalition pamphlet), the partner logo sits in the foot at --ems-u4 (24 px / 6 mm) right of the 561 mark, separated by an --ems-rule-hair vertical. Equal cap-heights.

Check
Photocopy at default settings. The mark must remain legible after one generation; the bracket rule must not break.

Vertical (A)

Inline (B)

Numeral and "GROUP" share a baseline. Separator: a vertical rule of weight --ems-rule-hair (1 px / 0.25 mm) at height equal to numeral cap-height, offset --ems-u3 (14 px / 3.5 mm) from each side. "GROUP" cap-height = 0.21 H, tracking +0.20 em.

Use in letterheads, document footers, web headers below 96 px tall, and sign-offs at the close of long-form text.

Foot (C)

Single line: 561 · GROUP. Family Space Grotesk 500 (not 700), tracking +0.16 em, separator middle-dot (U+00B7) padded by single space on each side. No bracket rule.

Use only in artifact feet, page-numbered colophons, and on objects where the vertical lockup will not survive the medium (single-color stamps, single-line signage, embroidered labels).

Wordmark "GROUP"

"GROUP" is not part of the primary mark; it is a permitted lockup partner. The mark may appear without "GROUP" (banners, stencils, stamps). "GROUP" never appears without the mark.

Translation of "GROUP" is permitted in the inline and foot lockups; the vertical lockup retains the English wordmark to keep the geometric block consistent. Translated wordmarks: "GRUPO" (es), "GWOUP" (ht), "GROUPE" (fr) at the same metrics.

§ 02.3
Stamp variant — alarm, single-color
FIG 02.2 — Stamp variant. Outer frame: 4 pt rule. Inner frame: 1 pt rule, offset 5 mm. Numeral centered, separator rule between numeral and wordmark.
When required
Physical objects 561 produces or processes: shipped crates, supply boxes, distributed pamphlet covers, hand-stamped envelopes, doors of held space. The stamp asserts physical authorship.
When permitted

Only on physical objects, only as a single-color print or hand-stamp impression, only in --ems-color-alarm.

Forbidden in digital surfaces. Forbidden on certificates of any kind, including ones we make ourselves. Forbidden as a "verified by 561" mark — 561 does not certify.

Geometry

Square, side length S. Outer frame: --ems-rule-beam (3 px / 0.75 mm) inset --ems-u1 (4 px / 1 mm) from trim. Inner frame: --ems-rule-hair inset --ems-u2 (8 px / 2 mm) from outer frame.

Numeral cap-height: 0.42 S. Numeral baseline at vertical center of the inner frame. Separator rule: --ems-rule-hair, full inner-frame width, 0.10 S below numeral baseline. Wordmark "GROUP": cap-height 0.07 S, tracking +0.30 em, baseline 0.10 S below separator rule.

Color

--ems-color-alarm only. Single-color impression. Misregistration is a property of the medium and is not a defect at this scale.

If alarm ink is unavailable (e.g., field conditions), defer the stamp; do not substitute black.

Check
Hand-stamp on uncoated kraft at S = 50 mm. Mark must remain legible after five sequential re-inkings without re-charging the die.
§ 02.4
Contracted — sub-minimum scale
When permitted
When the artifact's available mark area is below the minimum (§ 02.1, H < 32 px screen / 8 mm print) and scaling up the artifact is not possible — favicons, app icons, embroidered tags, stamped hardware.
Form

Numeral only, no bracket rule, no wordmark. Family unchanged (Space Grotesk 700). Tracking opens to −0.02 em (less tight than primary, to keep glyphs distinct at small size).

Favicon: 32 × 32 px, numeral cap-height 22 px, optically centered. App icon: 1024 × 1024 px source, numeral cap-height 580 px, optically centered, on --ems-color-paper ground with no border.

Forbidden at contracted scale
Any rendering of the bracket rule, any rendering of "GROUP", any background other than ink or paper, any rounded-corner mask other than what the host OS imposes.
§ 02.5
Forbidden uses
Form
No outlines around the numeral. No drop shadows. No embossing, debossing simulation, foil simulation, or 3-D extrusion. No italics. No condensed or expanded variants. No alternate weights (Space Grotesk 500 numerals are not the mark). No script overlays.
Composition
No co-marks beside the numeral (561 / EFA, 561 · xyz). No taglines beneath the wordmark. No "EST. 2024" or other date stamps. No registered trademark or copyright symbols adjacent to the mark.
Surface
The mark is not applied to: corporate sponsor materials of any kind, fundraising "thermometer" graphics, certificates, awards, merchandise sold for profit, promotional swag distributed by non-561 organizations.
Check
If the mark photographs as "polished" rather than as a number written down, it is set wrong.

§ 03
COLOR
Two Inks. One Surface.

Four colors. Two are structural (ink, bone), two are functional (red, blue). The functional pair is rationed, not styled — each has one job and one job only.

Red flags hazard or urgency. Use it for: deadlines, refusals (the strikethrough in §06), the single word in the slogan that is currently most at stake. Don't use it for emphasis, for headers, for "look here," for ornament, or for more than one word in any block of text.

Blue flags evidence. Use it for: figure callouts (FIG 03.x), data quantities, source links, dates of record. Don't use it as a body link color, as a fill, or anywhere a sentence isn't pointing at a fact.

Ink and bone are not interchangeable. Bone is paper; ink is mark. Don't tint either, don't add a third neutral, don't introduce gray. If you need recessive type, use ink at 60% (token --ink-soft). Once.

FOLIO 011
§ 04
TYPE
Sans & Slab.

Three families, each with one job. Space Grotesk sets all display, the mark, and UI labels. Roboto Slab sets all body copy and the slogan. JetBrains Mono sets all metadata — folios, captions, dates, filenames, tabular numerics.

The slab does the body because the body of the work should read with weight, not flow. Slab feet brake the eye — useful when sentences are short and you want each one to land. Don't substitute a humanist serif; the page reads as editorial, which is the wrong register.

Never mix weights within a family on the same line. Use weight to mark hierarchy, not emphasis. For emphasis inside body, use the slab's italic — sparingly. Bold inside body is a smell.

§ 05
GRID
Twelve Columns.

A different grid for each surface. The shared rule is the spacing scale; the column counts and gutters change because a tabloid is not a phone.

Spacing scale (units of 4): 4, 8, 14, 24, 48, 80, 120 px (or pt). Use these and only these. Anything between them is improvisation, and improvisation gets noted in the colophon.

Rule weights: hairline 1px (default), structural 3px (paragraph dividers, section heads), heavy 8px (the slogan band, mastheads). Don't introduce 2px or 4px.

Why a 12-column digital and an 8-column tabloid? 12 divides cleanly into 1/2/3/4/6 — useful for web. Tabloid newsprint reads at arm's length and 8 columns at 22pc each gives 50–60ch measure, which is what newsprint body has been since the 1920s. Don't fight the medium.

FOLIO 016
§ 06
VOICE
Plain. Specific.

Three rules carry most of the weight. Be specific. Three acres beats “our region.” Use verbs. “We grow,” not “we are growers.” Refuse the philanthropic register — no impact, no mission, no stakeholders, no community.

Scope. These shortcuts carry the compact display voice. For corporate articles and explanatory pages, the developed-prose procedure takes precedence over this section's short-sentence, exact-number, and word-list prescriptions. Preserve the argument, explain its terms, and give the reader enough evidence and context. A precise use of “community” or a necessary longer explanation is not a defect.

Sentences are short because readers are tired. Numbers are exact because vague numbers read as marketing. Names are real because anonymous “kin” reads like a stock photo. If you don't have a real number or a real name yet, leave the slot empty rather than filling it with mood.

FOLIO 018
§ 07
IMAGERY
Two Registers.

Two registers, no third. Commissioned artwork for the human register; photography, treated as evidence, for the documentary register. The bible governs only how each is reproduced — not who, when, or whether. Sourcing, rights, and consent are organizational concerns, not visual ones.

§ 07.1
Commissioned artwork — reproduction
Form
Hand-cut or hand-drawn. Visible tool marks are the medium speaking and are not corrected. Reproduced at no more than two inks (§ 04). Permitted ink combinations: ink + paper, ink + alarm, ink + witness. Three-ink artwork is rejected at proofing.
Source files
Retain the artist's full-resolution scan or vector. Reproduction artifacts (halftone, line-screen) are applied at layout time, not baked into the source.
§ 07.2
Photography — reproduction treatment
Treatment A — Duotone
Two inks, ground in paper. Ink + witness for documentary subjects; ink + alarm permitted only when alarm reading is part of the argument. Halftone screen 65 lpi for newsprint, 100 lpi for offset, 133 lpi for fine offset. Compress tonal range to the medium; do not chase shadow detail the press cannot hold.
Treatment B — Line-screen
Single ink, horizontal or vertical line screen, 25–45 lines per inch. Use for newsprint at small scale and for any photograph reproduced in alarm (alarm halftones become illegible).
Treatment C — Dot-screen
Single ink, coarse dot screen, 20–35 lines per inch. Use at poster scale where the dot itself is part of the read at distance.
Forbidden
Four-color process. Any color cast outside the system inks (§ 04). Filters, "instant" presets, simulated film grain. Vignettes. Burned or dodged edges beyond the photographer's print. Cropping that changes meaning.
Frame
The grid (§ 06) governs the frame. Photographs crop to grid columns.
§ 07.3
Caption — setting
Form
FIG ##.# — [what]. [who]. [where]. [when]. [source / rights].
Periods between fields. Empty fields drop.
Type
Roboto Slab 11/14, color --ems-color-ink-soft. FIG ##.# set in the same family at the same size, weight 700. Left-aligned to the figure's left edge; max width equals the figure's width. See any figure caption in this bible (e.g. FIG 03.1, folio 008) for the canonical setting.

TEMPLATES
Five Starting Files.

Five starting files. Each is a structure, not a finished piece. Copy is left as [placeholder]; fill before printing or publishing. Each template specifies trim, bleed, fold, paper, ink, and source file location.

Templates are the floor, not the ceiling. The bible mandates the spec; the editor of an artifact decides whether to follow this template, fork it, or write a new one. New templates earn a § 08.N entry only after the council has approved a third use.

Slogan set in Space Grotesk 700, four lines, one word per line, baseline-to-baseline 0.85 of cap-height. Line three (or the load-bearing line) carries alarm; all other lines carry ink. Mast: a single rule (--ems-rule-bar) at the top, with foot mark and version stamp in mono caption type.

The foot carries date, place of issue, and printer's name in JetBrains Mono 9 pt, tracking +0.18 em.

Test
Read the four slogan lines aloud at three meters in a hallway. Each line must read as one word, not as fragments. If any line breaks visually mid-word at that distance, the leading is too tight or the cap-height too small — increase by one step on the scale and retest.

FOLIO 024
§ 09
REFUSALS
Visual Moves We Don't Make.

Refusals about visual moves the bible can detect — not policy. Each carries a check the artifact can fail before it prints. Org-level refusals (clients, vocabulary, sourcing) live in 561's operating documents, not here.

§ 09.1
No philanthropic visual language
Refusal
Soft-focus subjects looking grateful or in need. White display type set over a tinted photograph. Mission-statement copy paired with smiling-face composition. The visual grammar of the donor appeal is forbidden.
Check
Cover the artifact's text. If what remains looks like a fundraising mailer, reset the photograph to evidentiary register (§ 07.2) or remove imagery.
§ 09.2
No agritech minimalism
Refusal
Thin geometric sans on green gradient. Aerial drone footage of fields. The visual grammar of the agriculture-tech pitch deck is forbidden.
Check
If the palette and type would be at home on a Series A landing page for a vertical-farming startup, return to the system palette (§ 04) and verify the type stack (§ 05).
§ 09.3
No type set over photography
Refusal
Photographs do not serve as backgrounds for display type. A caption beneath a photograph is permitted; a headline over a photograph is not.
Check
Confirm no display-scale type (above 18 pt) overlaps any photograph's bounding box.
§ 09.4
No AI-generated imagery
Refusal
No diffusion-model imagery, no GAN-generated portraiture, no AI-assisted illustration that produces final pixels without a credited artist's hand. Generative-AI tools used for sketching or reference are permitted; their output is not.
Check
For every figure, name the human author and the medium. If the answer is a model name, pull the figure.
§ 09.5
No co-marks beside the numeral
Refusal
No 561 / EFA, 561 · [partner], certification badges, or funder lockups beside the mark. Partner credits live in the colophon (§ 11) at body weight.
Check
Apply § 02.1 clearspace. If any other mark falls inside that box, move it to the colophon.
§ 09.6
No metallic, foil, emboss, or simulated 3-D finishes
Refusal
The mark and any 561 artifact is never metallic, foil-stamped, embossed, debossed, varnished, or 3-D rendered. The grammar of the bank, the diploma, and the corporate annual report is exactly what the bible refuses.
Check
Photocopy the artifact in single-color B&W. If information is lost (foil, emboss, gradient, drop shadow), the artifact has failed.
§ 09.7
No third-party requests on web surfaces
Refusal
No analytics scripts, tracking pixels, third-party fonts that phone home, third-party embeds, or advertising on any 561 web surface.
Check
Load the page with developer tools open. Network must show requests to one origin only — the 561 domain. Storage must show no cookies set.
FOLIO 026
§ 10
LINEAGE
Two Practices. Cited.

This bible follows the framework of visual engineering as articulated by emsenn — design that orients before it interprets. The visual language draws on two design lineages.

LEAD · BRUTALISM

Anti-ornament, hard hierarchy, exposed structure. Visible in the masthead, the section heads, the rule weights, the refusal of decoration. The page tells you it is a page.

LEAD · TUFTE

Evidence-first, attention budget honored, micro/macro reading. Visible in the caption convention, the table of color-budget rules, and the forensic register applied to records.

ALSO IN THE ROOM

Constructivist agitprop — El Lissitzky, Rodchenko, Stepanova. Diagonal hierarchy, type-as-construction, photomontage as argument. We use the methods, not the motifs.

Black radical print culture — Emory Douglas in the Black Panther, the broadsheets of the Liberation News Service, the mimeo and xerox lineage of movement printing. Cheap to make, fast to distribute, hard to misread.

PRIMARY METHODOLOGICAL SOURCE
emsenn — Visual Engineering Practices.
emsenn.net / library / engineering / design / visual-engineering
Cited as the methodological frame for this bible.
