# Voice substitution

Replace empty phrases with specific claims at proof reading, while preserving
the author's meaning and the amount of explanation the reader needs.

## Scope

The compact-copy procedure below carries the bible's authored display voice:
slogans, headings, captions, calls to action, and similarly brief body copy.
Keep its typographic force and refusal of the donor-appeal register.

In every format, a substitution must preserve the claim's meaning and status.
A stated commitment cannot become an accomplished result or a new promise;
necessary uncertainty and safety-preserving anonymity remain intact.

For corporate articles, essays, research explanations, and explanatory pages,
use [developed public prose](#developed-public-prose) for explanatory body prose.
The compact-copy procedure and line-by-line checks do not govern that prose. Its test
is whether an outside reader can follow the argument and examine the evidence.
Apply the visual rules to its presentation without turning a developed
explanation into a poster or a set of work notes.

## Compact-copy canonical pairs

| Don't                              | Instead                                  |
|------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| empowering communities             | name who, doing what                     |
| our impact this year               | a number, a date, a place                |
| stakeholders, beneficiaries        | people. by name when you can.            |
| we are committed to                | we did. we will. we won't.               |
| solutions, ecosystems, journeys    | use the actual word                      |

These are the bible's seed pairs.
Add to the table when a new phrase has been caught in proof twice; the [bible-as-living-document](../../../../glossary/terms/bible-as-living-document.md) discipline requires that the table grows from observed drift, not from speculation.

## Compact-copy procedure

1. Read every line of body and display copy.
2. For every line, ask: does this line make a specific claim — a name, a number, a date, a place, a verb attached to a subject — or does it gesture at a feeling?
3. If the line gestures at a feeling, find the underlying claim and substitute.
  If the underlying claim is not yet known, leave the slot empty rather than fill it with mood.
4. Re-read after substitution.
  The substituted line should be *shorter* and *more specific* than the original.

## The three rules at compact-copy proof time

The three rules of [voice-as-specificity](../../../../glossary/terms/voice-as-specificity.md) are operationalized here.

- **Be specific.** "Three acres" replaces "our region."
  A name replaces "kin."
  A date replaces "soon."
- **Use verbs.** "We grow" replaces "we are growers."
  "We did.
  We will.
  We won't." replaces "we are committed to."
- **Refuse the philanthropic register.** No *impact*, no *mission*, no *stakeholders*, no *community*.

## Empty slots in compact copy

When the underlying claim is not yet known, the bible's discipline is to *leave the slot empty* — set the line as `[name pending]` or `[date pending]` and ship the artifact only when the slot fills.
A sentence padded with mood to fill an empty slot is a defect that survives every subsequent edit cycle.

## Developed public prose

Begin with the person who will read the piece and the question it answers.
A visitor should not need the author's task history, office vocabulary, or
repository layout to understand why the subject matters. Keep that editorial
orientation in the working draft; the published opening should address the
subject itself.

Build the explanation the subject requires. Give the reader a claim or a
question, show the mechanism or reasoning behind it, and develop an example,
observation, or source they can examine. Explain the consequence. An essay may
need several such movements; a service page may need only a paragraph. This is
a test of completeness, not a mandatory section template.

Preserve the original ideas, disagreements, political commitments, imagery,
cadence, and useful technical depth. Where Black liberation, Indigenous
sovereignty, or long-horizon prosperity belongs to the argument, explain its
relation to the work rather than substituting a generic promise to make
things better. State an ambition as an ambition and a demonstrated result
as a result.

Introduce an unfamiliar term where its meaning becomes necessary. Keep a
precise scientific or institutional term when it does useful work; explain
what it denotes and use it consistently. Words such as *community*, *mission*,
*ecosystem*, and *solution* may name a real group, stated purpose, ecological
relation, or mathematical result. Judge the claim and its context rather than
rejecting the word. Preserve quotations and a group's own names for itself.

Use sources to support factual claims and distinguish observation, inference,
proposal, and intention where the distinction matters. Retain the precision
the evidence supports: an estimate must remain an estimate. Do not invent a
number, achievement, quotation, commitment, or named beneficiary to satisfy
the compact-copy rule. If a necessary fact is missing, resolve it before
publication or state the specific uncertainty when that uncertainty is part
of what the reader needs to know.

Read the piece aloud as prose. Vary sentence length where the reasoning needs
it, connect paragraphs, and remove repetition, inflated phrasing, and
unnecessary qualifications. A revision may grow when it supplies a missing
explanation. Added passages should develop the reader's understanding or the
piece's intended effect. Remove filler without stripping deliberate rhythm,
imagery, or emphasis.

At the existing editorial review or authorized final author pass, read the
piece without relying on the work thread. Can a reader say what it argues, follow its
important terms, and locate the support for its consequential claims? Replace
unexplained status labels, handoff instructions, and references to internal
work with the explanation the audience needs. Keep a technical detail when
it belongs to the subject; do not keep it merely because it was useful during
production. This review uses the existing editorial and publication authority;
it creates no new office or approval chain.

## Compact-copy check

For the compact-copy scope above, after substitution every line should:

- Be set in [Roboto Slab](../elements/type-stack.md) at B1 or larger.
- Carry at least one verb, one specific noun, and (where appropriate) at least one number, name, or date.
- Read shorter than the original.
- Not contain *impact*, *mission*, *stakeholder*, *community*, *empower*, *commit*, *solution*, *ecosystem*, *journey*.
