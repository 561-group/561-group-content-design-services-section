# Two registers

Imagery in the bible is governed by two registers and no third.

**Commissioned artwork — the human register.** Hand-cut or hand-drawn.
Visible tool marks are the medium speaking and are not corrected.
Reproduced at no more than two inks (see [color-budget](color-budget.md)).
Permitted ink combinations: ink + paper, ink + alarm, ink + witness.
Three-ink artwork is rejected at proofing.
The artist's full-resolution scan or vector is retained as the source file; reproduction artifacts (halftone, line-screen) are applied at layout time, not baked into the source.
See [commissioned-artwork](../../documents/articles/specifications/elements/commissioned-artwork.md).

**Photography, treated as evidence — the documentary register.** Reproduced as duotone, line-screen, or dot-screen depending on press and scale (see [duotone-treatment](../../documents/articles/specifications/procedures/duotone-treatment.md), [line-screen-treatment](../../documents/articles/specifications/procedures/line-screen-treatment.md), [dot-screen-treatment](../../documents/articles/specifications/procedures/dot-screen-treatment.md)).
Compress tonal range to the medium; do not chase shadow detail the press cannot hold.
Cropping that changes meaning, four-color process, color casts outside the system inks, filters, simulated film grain, vignettes, and burned or dodged edges beyond the photographer's print are all forbidden.
See [photograph-treatment](../../documents/articles/specifications/elements/photograph-treatment.md).

The two registers are not stylistic choices among many; they are the only two registers the bible recognizes.
A third register (collage, illustration that is neither commissioned-handcraft nor evidence-photograph, generative imagery, stock illustration) is a defect at proofing.

The bible governs only how each register is reproduced — *not who, when, or whether*.
Sourcing, rights, and consent are organizational concerns and live in 561's operating documents, not here.
The visual register's discipline is the bible's job; the question of which artist or which subject is the work's job.

Captions are the bridge between the two registers and the rest of the artifact.
Every figure carries the same five-field [caption-as-evidence](caption-as-evidence.md) setting.
