# Bible as living document

The bible is a working manual, not a finished system. § 00 names this on the first inside page; § 11 reasserts it on the last.
The status field of every edition reads "Living."
The colophon's "next review" field reads: *when something it says fails*.
There is no scheduled revision cadence.

Three commitments follow.

1. **Edits welcome.** Where the bible fails the work, propose an edit.
  Bring the failure with the edit — an example of the artifact the bible's current rule produced, and the artifact the edited rule would produce instead.
2. **Where the bible contradicts the work, the work wins.** The bible is downstream of the work.
  A working artifact that the bible cannot describe is evidence that the bible needs editing, not that the artifact is wrong.
  Then come fix the bible so it stops failing the next person.
3. **Reproduction is open; resale is forbidden; translation is encouraged; simplification is not.** The bible may be photocopied, re-typeset, hosted on any 561 surface, and translated into any language a contributor reads.
  Compressing the bible into a one-page "summary" is forbidden — the rules do not survive simplification.

The "living document" stance is what makes the bible's other concepts coherent.
[Refusal as design method](refusal-as-design-method.md) requires that new refusals can be added as new drifts are observed.
[Template as floor](template-as-floor.md) requires that templates can be forked and that successful forks earn promotion.
Without the living-document commitment, the bible would calcify into the kind of brand-guide PDF it explicitly is not.
